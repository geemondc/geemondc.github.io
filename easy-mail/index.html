Easier ways

Talk, listen, and take your time

Typing is optional. These two guides use tools already built into the iPhone and iPad.

Voice · 1

Speak instead of typing

✍️Open Write
🎤Tap the microphone
💬Talk slowly
  1. Tap “Write an email.” Then tap inside the message area so the keyboard appears.
  2. Tap the microphone on the keyboard. On many iPhones it is beside the space bar or in the lower-right corner.
  3. Speak one short sentence at a time. Say “period” for a period and “new line” to start a new line.
  4. Tap the microphone again to stop. Read the words before sending. Dictation is helpful, but it can mishear names.
First-time setup: Settings → General → Keyboard → turn on Enable Dictation.
Voice · 2

Hear my mail read to me

✉️Open a message
☝️☝️Swipe down with two fingers
🔊Listen
  1. Open the email you want to hear.
  2. Swipe down with two fingers from the very top of the screen. This is called Speak Screen.
  3. Use the small listening controller to pause, continue, or stop.
  4. For one sentence: press and hold to select it, then tap Speak in the small menu.
First-time setup: Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → turn on Speak Screen and Speak Selection.

The golden rule: official apps only
  1. Download the official app from the App Store and check the publisher name.
  2. Sign in inside that app. Never type the password into EasyMail or any helper page.
  3. Open the inbox once and confirm the newest messages appear.
  4. Add EasyMail to the Home Screen using the steps below.
Gmail
  1. Install and open the Gmail app by Google.
  2. Sign in inside Gmail. Finish any Google safety check there.
  3. Tap the Gmail tile above to confirm it reaches the inbox.
Yahoo · BellSouth · AT&T
  1. For Carol, start with the Yahoo Mail app. BellSouth mail is handled through Yahoo/AT&T.
  2. Sign in inside Yahoo Mail with the full BellSouth address.
  3. Prefer Yahoo Mail over Apple Mail for the first setup. It avoids the separate AT&T secure mail key step.
  4. If Apple Mail is required: AT&T may require a free “secure mail key.” Create it in the AT&T account profile, then enter that key — not the normal password — inside Apple Mail’s account setup. Never enter it on EasyMail.
AOL Mail
  1. Install and open the official AOL app.
  2. Sign in inside AOL and confirm the inbox opens.
  3. Use the AOL tile above for the official web inbox when needed.
Outlook
  1. Install and open Microsoft Outlook.
  2. Add the account inside Outlook and finish Microsoft’s sign-in there.
  3. Use the Outlook tile above for the official web inbox.
Apple Mail
  1. Open Settings → Apps → Mail → Mail Accounts on newer iPhones, or Settings → Mail → Accounts on older versions.
  2. Add the account there and sign in with the provider.
  3. Open the blue Mail icon from the Home Screen to read mail. Apple Mail has no inbox web link.
Put EasyMail on the Home Screen
  1. Open EasyMail in Safari.
  2. Tap Share — the square with an arrow pointing up.
  3. Tap “Add to Home Screen.”
  4. Name it “EasyMail” and tap Add. It now opens like an app, with one big icon.

Make the phone kinder to shaky hands

Set these once. Start gently — too much Hold Duration can make ordinary taps feel slow.

Bigger, bolder words

  1. Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size.
  2. Turn on Bold Text.
  3. Tap Larger Text and move the slider right.

Forgive extra taps

  1. Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Touch Accommodations.
  2. Turn it on. Try Hold Duration at 0.30 seconds.
  3. Turn on Ignore Repeat at about 0.30 seconds.

A floating Home helper

  1. Settings → Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch.
  2. Turn it on and keep the menu simple: Home, Siri, and Control Center.
  3. Practice opening it together before leaving.

Gary’s five-minute test

  • The correct inbox opens from the provider tile.
  • A blank email opens from “Write an email.”
  • Dictation writes one short sentence correctly.
  • Speak Screen reads one message out loud.
  • The EasyMail Home Screen icon brings you back here.
Pause and check

If mail looks strange, do not tap in a hurry

Unexpected messages can pretend to be a bank, a delivery company, or even family. Do not tap strange links, do not send money, and do not share codes. Close the message and ask Gary, family, or staff to look with you.
