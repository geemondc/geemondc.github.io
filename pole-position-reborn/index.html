PREPARE TO QUALIFY

One timed qualifying lap at Fuji Speedway sets your place on the 8-car grid. Beat 58.50s and you take POLE POSITION. Run out of time and it's game over — then the Grand Prix: 4 laps, 7 rivals, thread past them all.

How to play: Qualify first. Shift LOW → HIGH once you're rolling (HIGH bogs down at low speed — downshift for tight corners and after any crash). Rival cars hold their lines and drift across the straights; pass them clean. Touch a car or a sign at speed and your car explodes — a replacement rolls out, and the clock never stops.

What “arcade-exact” means here — the documented values used

Structure: one qualifying lap against a 90-second clock → grid of 8 → Grand Prix of 4 laps of Fuji Speedway (factory-default race length) against 7 rivals, with time extended at each lap line. Qualifying brackets (seconds): 58.50 / 62.50 / 66.50 / 70.50 / 74.50 / 78.50 / 82.50 / 90.00 for grid slots 1–8, with the fastest bracket announced as POLE POSITION, as in the arcade. Gearbox: two-speed LOW/HIGH shifter; LOW tops at 160 km/h, HIGH at 244 km/h, and HIGH bogs at low speed. Scoring: finish points 15,000 / 12,000 / 10,000 / 8,000 / 6,000 / 4,000 / 2,000 / 1,000 for 1st–8th, 50 points per rival passed, 200 points per remaining second at the finish. Honest note: these are the widely published operator-manual / reference values for the 1982 Namco original; the qualifying-bonus scale and per-lap extension seconds vary by DIP-switch setting on real cabinets, so this build uses the standard published brackets and awards time per lap so a ~60-second lap pace keeps the race alive exactly as the default cabinet does.
